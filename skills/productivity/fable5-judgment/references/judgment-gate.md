# Judgment Gate

Use this before finalizing a Fable 5-led answer.

## Decisive Issue Check

- What is the real decision or review question?
- Which facts would change the recommendation?
- Which parts are evidence, inference, preference, or speculation?
- Which stakeholder, time horizon, or downside case is easiest to ignore?
- Is there an unexamined alternative that dominates the current options?

## Evidence Check

- Did Fable 5 own the judgment-dependent discovery needed to stabilize the
  frame, or explicitly define coverage and source-selection rules before bulk
  collection began?
- Was each delegated lane justified by independence, context isolation, fresh
  verification, tool fit, or a measured cost/latency advantage?
- Did workers inspect primary or source-of-truth material for decisive claims?
- Are current facts dated and sourced?
- Are local code/file claims tied to paths and line references?
- Are weak sources, stale facts, and conflicts labeled instead of smoothed over?

## Execution Check

- Could each worker execute from a stable packet without inventing a missing
  premise or decision rule?
- When evidence broke a premise or exposed a new consequential choice, was that
  judgment returned to Fable 5 before execution continued?
- Is every progress or completion claim backed by a tool result, inspected
  source, test, or named artifact from this run?
- Did a consequential long run receive an independent specification check when
  anchoring or drift was a material risk?
- Did the lead avoid unnecessary delegation and idle waiting?
- Were evidence and concise rationale requested instead of private reasoning or
  a chain-of-thought transcript?

## Synthesis Check

- Did Fable 5 personally resolve conflicts instead of averaging worker opinions?
- Is the final recommendation tied to a decision rule?
- Is the confidence level earned by the evidence?
- Is the caveat concrete enough to act on?
- Is there a clear condition that would change the recommendation?

## Final Answer Shape

For decisions:

1. Recommendation first.
2. Why this is the best call.
3. Decisive evidence.
4. Main caveat or risk.
5. What would change the answer.

For reviews:

1. Highest-impact finding first.
2. Evidence and why it matters.
3. Missed issue or hidden assumption.
4. Suggested fix or next evidence wave.

For research synthesis:

1. Answer first.
2. Claim table or concise evidence trail.
3. Conflicts and source quality.
4. Remaining uncertainty.
